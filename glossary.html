MATERIALS

Magneton metals

Three metals support the new world: durable bronze-like Type A, green opposite-polarity Type B, and rare silvery Type C. Rovan alone knows the crafting secret behind his five field-drifting honour guards.

The three types.

The metal of the hull.

A bronze-like metal prized for its durability. It is the most common form of Magneton and is used to build ship hulls.

CRECENTLY DISCOVERED

Type C

Silver that changes the balance of power.

The most malleable type of Magneton, and the most difficult to find. This recently discovered silver metal is impenetrable and impervious to weapons. It can drift along the magnetic fields; Rovan alone knows how to craft it into seamless armour and magnetic weapons that give his honour guard this advantage.

Rovan’s five.

The stolen Type C yields exactly five sets for five honour-guard captains. Their practical, workshop-made silver armour has no joints or gaps and is impervious to weapons. Crimson cloth is worn over the continuous metal. Narrow brass edging and small fittings finish the suits; crimson enamel badges carry Veymarch’s silver broken shield and star. Rovan alone knows the crafting secret; he has no material for a sixth set.

The Argonaut’s refit

Rovan puts his newfound wealth into his existing capital ship: a wedge-shaped armoured prow, crimson side plating, huge twin-barrel heavy cannon turrets, upgraded engines and magnetic stabilisers. The engineers finish the work in fourteen days. Its hull remains durable bronze Type A; green Type B powers the machinery. The five captains keep the scarce Type C.

PEOPLE

People of the Prologue

Duke Rovan Vey

A protector whose sacrifices left his own city poor. He turns seized resources into an army, captures an Auric Reach city, and secretly forges five silver honour-guard sets.

General Grievous

Rovan’s right-hand man, sharing his fleet’s hardships and planning how to hold the captured city.

The five honour guards

Five captains equipped with practical, seamless Type C armour and five distinct magnetic-weapon loadouts. The silver resists every weapon and allows them to drift between ships on magnetic fields. Their titles are Stormlance, Windblade, Bulwark, Riftwalker and Hullbreaker. There is no metal for a sixth set.

Admiral Joseph

The arrogant Auric Reach commander leads three Bastions after Rovan. Hoping victory will win him a duchy of his own, he dismisses his sailors’ warnings and orders the pursuit into the Shattered Plains. His fleet surrenders on page19. Rovan sentences him to be thrown overboard for his arrogant command; the sentence is shown, not its execution.

The Shattered Plains

A stretch of sky crowded with enormous floating rocks and very strong magnetic fields. Flying here is difficult. Rovan chooses it so the three enemy Bastions must separate to follow the Argonaut between the rocks.

Three prizes

The first capture of enemy capital ships in the shattered world’s history ends with three taken in one battle. Josephine, Serenity and Serendipity surrender to Rovan. Prize crews take their bridges while repair gangs tend the damaged engines. Rovan now owns four capital ships including his original Argonaut; the prizes need repairs immediately after the battle. At New Frontier on page20, repairs and repainting prepare all four to sail for Oram.

New Frontier and Oram

New Frontier

Rovan renames the conquered Auric harbour as his foothold for the campaign. Volunteers arrive from Auric Reach and Veymarch while crews repair the captured ships and repaint their hull panels crimson, keeping the bronze ribs and silver Veymarch markings.

Oram

The capital of Auric Reach, and the largest and richest city in the world through access to most farmland. The four-ship fleet leaves New Frontier on page20 and besieges Oram on page21. Convoys supply the fleet outside the city. Its people, unused to danger, panic as the cannons fire. On page22, Duke Ferdinand confers with his barons and surrenders the city.

The siege of Oram

Oram’s vast agricultural shelves support its wealth and sprawling city. Four Veymarch capitals hold siege positions outside it, supported by smaller trading convoys from New Frontier. The city flies Auric Reach’s gold and navy banners. Continued bombardment brings Duke Ferdinand and his barons to surrender Oram on page22.

Duke Ferdinand

The ruler of Auric Reach, previously unnamed. He wears a gold mantle over navy court dress. Under continued bombardment, he confers with his barons and surrenders Oram to Rovan. The surrender concerns the city; no fate for the rest of Auric Reach or detailed terms has been established.
