# Magneton — world reference

Magneton is a working title taken from the central resource. This document records the world premise; it does not establish a protagonist or main plot.

## Established premise

- Nuclear war shattered Earth into floating islands.
- Magnetic waves hold the fragments together. The fields are turbulent but have predictable patterns, allowing humans to navigate them.
- Humanity was nearly wiped out. Over generations, survivors evolved new attributes under the influence of the fields. The exact attributes are not yet defined.
- The fusion of nuclear materials produced Magneton, a new metal with extraordinary magnetic properties.
- Humans use oppositely polarized metal components in magnetic motors to generate power. Magneton technology keeps cities and islands afloat and creates a local field that reproduces the old experience of gravity.
- Magneton became essential to survival, energy, construction, and transport.
- Miners extracted Magneton and built mining and trading ships from it. Their magnetic motors let them travel through the fields between cities and mining sites.
- Control of extraction, ships, and trade turned the miners into ruling barons. Floating cities gathered into duchies, forming a new feudal society.

## Opening comic

1. The fracture: nuclear war, floating islands, near extinction.
2. Adaptation: humanity changes; Magneton is discovered.
3. The cities: motors, power, flotation, and restored local gravity.
4. The barons: mining fleets, resource control, and duchies.

## Ship design and classes

Ships follow a Zeppelin silhouette: elongated rigid metal hulls, rounded noses, structural ribs, tail fins, portholes, exposed machinery, and Magneton-powered motors. The user's visual reference establishes an industrial copper-and-iron design language. These are magnetically powered craft; their shape does not establish balloon lift as a world rule.

| Class | Scale | Purpose and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mining ships | Small | Compact working ships with large holds for storing and transporting ore. |
| Schooners | Small | Highly mobile commuter vessels for travel within different parts of a city or duchy. |
| Trading ships | Large | Carry freight and passengers; difficult to navigate because of their size and bulk. |
| Men-of-war | Capital | The most expensive and rarest ships. Many duchies may have no more than three. |

The fleet illustrations explore these silhouettes. Exact dimensions, crew counts, armaments, and engine specifications remain open.

## Proposed ship variants

The glossary extends the four established classes with design studies. These names and layouts are proposals for development, not additional story events or numerical specifications.

- **Mole**: squat mining ship with a deep ore cassette and drill.
- **Needle**: light, highly agile commuter schooner.
- **Caravan**: twin-hull freight and passenger trader.
- **Bastion**: broad armored capital ship with multiple keels.
- **Kestrel**: small open-deck commuter inspired by the second reference.
- **Terrace**: large merchant with terraced passenger decks and an aft bridge, also inspired by the second reference.

The newer reference introduces open decks, a swept lower hull, aft superstructures, and hull-edge machinery as alternative silhouettes alongside rigid Zeppelins. Magnetic field rings remain the shared propulsion language. The capital-ship scarcity applies to the entire class: variants do not each receive a separate three-ship allowance.

## Magneton types — established material lore

| Type | Appearance | Properties and availability | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Bronze-like metal | Very durable; the most common type | Ship hulls |
| B | Glowing green, in both opposing polarities | Opposite-polarity forms; availability not yet specified | Delicate machinery and hull components |
| C | Colour not specified | Most malleable; most difficult to find; recently discovered | Armour and swords |

Type B's hull-component use is retained from the user's description; the exact division between Type A hull structure and Type B components is not yet defined. Type C has no established colour or additional supernatural properties. The two opposing Type B cores should both appear green, not as different-coloured metals.
